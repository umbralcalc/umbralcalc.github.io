# How do we prioritise human alignment in the age of AI?
<div style="height:0.75em;"></div>

We build systems of agent-human interaction which prioritise human alignment, but what does this this mean in practice?

Human conversation is critical to [context gathering and alignment](#ref-clarkbrennan1991), however, the [introduction of agentic chats into individual human interactions](#ref-hancock2020) has changed the system.

<center><img src="https://pub-afdb1348ec964ca5b530aa758c0bdc56.r2.dev/assets/umbralcalculations/agents-humans-interactions-model.svg" width=600/></center>

Humans must now align more than just themselves with others, they may also have to align with the agents which others chat to, as well as their own. 

This increased size of the system with siloed chat threads makes alignment between humans more difficult in principle, because there are simply more components to it and [not all of them have direct paths to communicate](#ref-bavelas1950) to one-another.

To resolve this issue, we can draw experience from how humans aligned before AI. We can introduce [shared artefacts such as diagrams and documents](#ref-stargriesemer1989) that all components of the system must interact and align over.

<center><img src="https://pub-afdb1348ec964ca5b530aa758c0bdc56.r2.dev/assets/umbralcalculations/agents-humans-artefacts-interactions-model.svg" width=600/></center>



The question this raises is: if we really want to prioritise _human_ alignment in this system, should we consider [removing the private AI chats altogether](#ref-shneidermanmaes1997)? 

Communication would still occur between all components of the system, however, their tendencies to reach misaligned outcomes are [controlled through this artefact](#ref-heylighen2016). Perhaps we should be designing applications this way?
